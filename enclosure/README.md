# Printed enclosure for the Pi 3B+ (stage 1, no sensor board)

Generated by `make_enclosure.py`; the STLs in `stl/` are ready to slice. Change a number at the
top of the script and re-run to regenerate. 149 × 82 × 30 mm overall, plus 5 mm glands on the
cable end and 12 mm mounting ears on one side.

The idea: **every plug is inside the box.** The Pi sits at one end. The other end is a cable bay
where the USB plugs sit; each cable is pinched by a clamp and leaves through a round exit in a
round gland boss. The micro-USB power plug sits in a channel along the Pi's long edge and its
lead runs along the channel to the same end. Nothing outside can be knocked or walk out.

## Parts

| File | What | Print |
|---|---|---|
| `base.stl` | Pi standoffs, cable channel, cable bay with three round exits and clamp cradles, intake vents low on both long walls, SD slot, two mounting ears, three lid posts | flat, no supports |
| `lid.stl` | alignment lip, exhaust vents over the processor, seven screw holes | upside down, no supports |
| `clamp_cap.stl` | 23 × 10 × 3 mm bar with a ridge that pinches the cable jacket onto its cradle | flat; print **3** |

Material: **ASA** (best in a hot footwell) or PETG; not PLA. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 30 % infill.
On the P1S: ASA with the door closed and the top glass on.

## Cable exits (bay end wall, looking at it from outside, left to right)

| Position | Bore | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| in line with the channel | 5.2 mm | power lead from the micro-USB plug |
| in line with USB stack 1 | 6.0 mm | the FTDI cable (K-line) |
| in line with USB stack 2 | 5.2 mm | spare: GPS puck or USB stick lead |

Measure your cables' outer diameter and set the bores 0.6–1.0 mm larger in `EXITS`. An unused
exit takes a short printed plug or a blob of silicone.

## Hardware

- 7 × M2.5 heat-set inserts (base standoffs and the three bay/channel posts); 4 × M2.5 × 30 mm
  screws through the lid posts and the Pi, 3 × M2.5 × 8 mm at the bay corners.
  (`INSERT = False` switches to self-tapping screws in 2.2 mm holes.)
- 5 × M3 heat-set inserts in the clamp posts (the two USB clamps share a post); 6 × M3 × 8 mm screws.
- Stick-on heatsinks on the SoC and the USB/Ethernet chip (in the PiShop set).

## Assembly

1. Heat-set the inserts (iron at ~250 °C, push gently and square).
2. Heatsinks on; Pi onto its standoffs, SD card end towards the slot.
3. Thread each cable in through its gland from outside, plug it into the Pi, lay it in its
   cradle and screw the clamp cap down until the jacket is just compressed. Micro-USB lead:
   plug in, run along the channel, out through its exit.
4. Lid on; seven screws. Snug, not tight.
5. Ears to the kick-panel bracket with cable ties or two 4 mm screws.

## Fit-check first

Print the base alone. Check: board drops onto the standoffs without force (else raise
`CLEARANCE`); USB plugs with their boots end before the clamp cradles (else raise `BAY_L`);
micro-USB plug fits in the channel (else raise `CHANNEL_W`); cables pass their bores. Then print
the set. This first version is drawn from published dimensions, not a physical check, so treat
the first print as the prototype.

## Cooling

Intake slots low on both long walls, under the board; exhaust slots in the lid over the processor;
convection does the rest with the heatsinks and the dashboard's light load. Expect about ambient
+20 °C at the chip; the Pi throttles at 80 °C. The dashboard logs CPU temperature, so this gets
measured. If it runs hot, a 30 mm fan boss is the next revision.
