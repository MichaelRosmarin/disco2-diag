# Printed enclosure for the Pi 3B+ (stage 1, no sensor board)

Generated by `make_enclosure.py`; the STLs in `stl/` are ready to slice. Change a number at the
top of the script and re-run to regenerate. 141 × 86 × 30 mm overall, plus 6 mm glands and 12 mm mounting ears on the GPIO side. Two parts, no clamp hardware.

The idea: **every plug is inside the box, and the lid is the clamp.** The Pi sits at one end. The
other end is a cable bay where the USB plugs sit; the OBD cable leaves through that end. The
micro-USB power plug sits in a channel along the Pi's long edge, and its lead runs along the channel
and leaves through the long wall on that side, so OBD and power come out of different sides of the
box. Each cable leaves through a round gland split on the parting line: the base carries the lower half-circle, the lid the upper half, and the bore is
0.4 mm under the cable diameter. Screwing the lid down compresses the jacket, so the seven lid
screws hold the cables as well as the lid. Inboard of the wall the lid has a short tunnel over each
cable, widened by 1.5 mm, so the cable can bend down to its plug without a sharp edge. The
micro-USB power plug sits in a channel along the Pi's long edge and its lead runs along the channel
to the same end. Nothing outside can be knocked or walk out on corrugations.

## Parts

| File | What | Print |
|---|---|---|
| `base.stl` | Pi standoffs, cable channel, cable bay, lower gland halves, intake vents low on both long walls, SD slot, two mounting ears, three lid posts | flat, no supports |
| `lid.stl` | alignment lip, upper gland halves with relieved tunnels, exhaust vents over the processor, seven screw holes | upside down (outside face on the bed), no supports |

Material: **ASA** (best in a hot footwell) or PETG; not PLA. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 30 % infill.
On the P1S: ASA with the door closed and the top glass on. The glands are the one place the
profile matters: slice the lid with the gland half-rings as printed arcs (they are; Bambu Studio
handles the overhang of the upper half-rings at this size without supports).

## Cable exits

| Wall | Position | Cable OD | Bore | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OBD end | in line with USB stack 1 | 5.0 mm | 4.6 mm | the FTDI cable (K-line) |
| OBD end | in line with USB stack 2 | 4.2 mm | 3.8 mm | spare: GPS puck or USB stick lead |
| power side (long wall) | 48 mm from the SD end | 4.2 mm | 3.8 mm | power lead from the micro-USB plug |

The power gland is 37 mm along the wall from the micro-USB socket on purpose: the lead leaves the
plug 2 mm above the board and the gland is 18 mm above it, so it needs that run to rise. Use a
straight micro-USB plug; the channel is 24 mm wide to give the lead room to turn along the wall.

Measure your cables with a vernier and put the real outer diameters in `EXITS`; the script takes
`PINCH` (0.4 mm) off for the bore. Too loose → the cable slides; too tight → the lid won't sit
flat on the wall. An unused exit takes a 6 mm length of any 4 mm cable, or a blob of silicone.

## Hardware

- 7 × M2.5 heat-set inserts (four base standoffs and the three bay/channel posts); 4 × M2.5 × 30 mm
  screws through the lid posts and the Pi, 3 × M2.5 × 8 mm at the bay corners.
  (`INSERT = False` switches to self-tapping screws in 2.2 mm holes.)
- Stick-on heatsinks on the SoC and the USB/Ethernet chip (in the PiShop set).

## Assembly

1. Heat-set the inserts (iron at ~250 °C, push gently and square).
2. Heatsinks on; Pi onto its standoffs, SD card end towards the slot.
3. Plug each USB cable into the Pi and lay it in its half-round seat in the end wall. Micro-USB
   lead: plug in, run it along the channel towards the OBD end, rising to its seat in the long wall.
4. Lid on, checking each cable sits in its upper half as the lid comes down; seven screws. Snug,
   not tight — the glands should just grip the jackets. Tug each cable: none should move.
5. Ears to the kick-panel bracket with cable ties or two 4 mm screws.

## Fit-check first

Print the base alone. Check: board drops onto the standoffs without force (else raise
`CLEARANCE`); USB plugs with their boots end before the wall with room to rise to the seats (else
raise `BAY_L`); micro-USB plug fits in the channel (else raise `CHANNEL_W`); each cable lies in
its seat with the top of the jacket a little proud of the wall. Then print the lid. This version is
drawn from published dimensions, not a physical check, so treat the first print as the prototype.

## Cooling

Intake slots low on both long walls, under the board; exhaust slots in the lid over the processor;
convection does the rest with the heatsinks and the dashboard's light load. Expect about ambient
+20 °C at the chip; the Pi throttles at 80 °C. The dashboard logs CPU temperature, so this gets
measured. If it runs hot, a 30 mm fan boss is the next revision.
