# Printed enclosure for the Pi 3B+ (stage 1, no sensor board)

Generated by `make_enclosure.py`; the STLs in `stl/` are ready to slice. Change a number at the
top of the script and re-run to regenerate. 92.8 × 63.8 × 30.2 mm overall, plus ears and bosses.

The point of the design: nothing on the outside can walk out on corrugations. The USB plugs
(FTDI cable, later the GPS and the USB stick) and the micro-USB power plug are held in by printed
clamp plates that bear on the plug overmolds; the FTDI dongle itself stays outside on its cable.

## Parts

| File | What | Print |
|---|---|---|
| `base.stl` | Pi on four standoffs, SD slot, low intake vents on both long walls, two ears for cable ties or 4 mm screws, bosses for the two clamps | flat, no supports |
| `lid.stl` | drops in on an alignment lip; four posts come down onto the Pi's holes; exhaust vents over the processor | upside down, no supports |
| `usb_clamp.stl` | plate across the Ethernet and USB plugs, 17 mm off the end wall | flat |
| `power_clamp.stl` | same for the micro-USB power plug | flat |

Material: **ASA** (best in a hot footwell) or PETG. Not PLA. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 30 % infill.
On the P1S: ASA with the door closed and the top glass on; PETG needs nothing special.

## Hardware

- 4 × M2.5 heat-set inserts (OD ~3.5 mm) into the base standoffs; 4 × M2.5 × 30 mm screws down
  through the lid posts and the Pi into them. (Set `INSERT = False` for self-tapping screws into
  2.2 mm holes instead; fine for a few open/close cycles, inserts for a product.)
- 4 × M3 heat-set inserts in the square pockets of the clamp bosses; 4 × M3 × 8 mm screws.
- Stick-on heatsinks on the SoC and the USB/Ethernet chip (in the PiShop set).

## Assembly

1. Heat-set the inserts (soldering iron at ~250 °C, push gently and square).
2. Pi onto the standoffs, SD card end towards the slot. Heatsinks on first.
3. Lid on; four M2.5 screws through the lid posts into the standoffs. Snug, not tight.
4. Plug in the FTDI cable (and anything else), slide the USB clamp down over the cables so its
   slots straddle them, screw to the bosses. Same for the power lead.
5. Ears to the kick-panel bracket with cable ties or two screws.

## Fit-check first

Print the base alone first. Check: board drops onto the standoffs without force (else raise
`CLEARANCE`), connectors centred in their openings (else shift the `*_OPENINGS` numbers), the
clamp plate lands behind your plugs' overmolds (else change `CLAMP_STANDOFF`: measure from the
wall face to the back of the plug's rubber body and add 1 mm). Then print the set.

## Cooling

Intake slots low on both long walls, exhaust slots in the lid over the processor: convection
through the case. With the heatsinks and the dashboard's light load, expect roughly ambient
+20 °C at the chip; the Pi throttles at 80 °C. The dashboard logs CPU temperature so this is
measured, not assumed. If it ever runs hot, a 30 mm fan boss is the next revision.
